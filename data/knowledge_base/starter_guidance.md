# USCIS EB-1A policy notes (starter corpus)

This file is a concise paraphrase for local development, not a substitute for the live Policy Manual. The official source is [6 USCIS-PM F.2, Extraordinary Ability](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-2). USCIS's October 2, 2024 clarification is [PA-2024-24](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/policy-manual-updates/20241002-ExtraordinaryAbility.pdf). For production use, refresh this corpus from the current USCIS source and retain source version/date metadata.

## Overall framework

The E11 extraordinary ability classification concerns people in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics. The evidentiary review has two stages. First, the petitioner must show a major internationally recognized award or documentation satisfying at least three of the ten regulatory criteria (or qualifying comparable evidence where the criteria do not readily apply). USCIS then evaluates the petition in its entirety in a final merits determination, considering whether the person has sustained national or international acclaim, recognized achievements, and is among the small percentage at the top of the field. Three checkmarks alone do not establish eligibility. Evidence quality, context, and significance matter.

## Criterion 1 — Awards

Evidence may concern nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field. Relevant questions include the award's scope, selection process, reputation, and whether it recognizes excellence in the person's field. USCIS clarified that qualifying team awards may be considered and that past awards are not excluded merely because they are not recent. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1); PA-2024-24]

## Criterion 2 — Membership

The association's membership must require outstanding achievements, as judged by recognized national or international experts in the relevant field. Ordinary membership, payment of dues, or experience-based admission alone may be insufficient. USCIS clarified that past memberships can be considered. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1); PA-2024-24]

## Criterion 3 — Published material about the person

The material must be about the person and relate to their work in the field; the source must qualify as a professional publication, major trade publication, or other major media. Capture title, author, date, outlet, and circulation or audience information where available. USCIS clarified that the material need not independently demonstrate the value of the person's work to satisfy the plain criterion. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1); PA-2024-24]

## Criterion 4 — Judging

Evidence should establish that the person participated individually or on a panel as a judge of others' work in the same or an allied field. Invitations alone may not establish completed participation; document the event, role, date, and work reviewed. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1)]

## Criterion 5 — Original contributions of major significance

The contribution should be original and of major significance to the field, not merely useful to one employer or described as innovative. Evidence may explain adoption, influence, citations, implementation, independent recognition, or field-level impact, supported by documentation. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1)]

## Criterion 6 — Scholarly authorship

Evidence concerns authorship of scholarly articles in the field in professional or major trade publications or other major media. Identify the work, authorship, publication venue, date, and why the venue and work qualify as scholarly. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1)]

## Criterion 7 — Artistic exhibitions or showcases

The criterion expressly concerns display of the person's work at artistic exhibitions or showcases. USCIS clarified that non-artistic exhibitions should only be considered through a properly supported comparable-evidence argument, not automatically as this criterion. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1); PA-2024-24]

## Criterion 8 — Leading or critical role

The role must be for an organization or establishment with a distinguished reputation. For a leading role, evidence should establish leadership within the organization. For a critical role, evidence should show contributions of significant importance to the outcome of the organization's activities. The role can be for a distinguished department or division, not only the entire organization. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1); PA-2022-12]

## Criterion 9 — High salary or remuneration

Evidence should show that the person's salary or other remuneration is significantly high compared with others working in the field. Provide reliable comparative compensation data for the relevant occupation, location, and period, along with pay records or contracts. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1)]

## Criterion 10 — Commercial success in performing arts

Evidence focuses on commercial success in the performing arts, shown by box-office receipts or record, cassette, compact disc, or video sales. Volume of sales or receipts should be placed in context relative to others in similar pursuits; the mere fact of performing or releasing a production does not by itself establish the criterion. [6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1)]

## Retrieval and evaluation cautions

These notes are intentionally brief and are not legal advice. The resume is not the full petition record. Treat unsupported or ambiguous resume statements as incomplete information; do not infer qualifying reputation, selectivity, significance, comparative standing, or acclaim without evidence. Cite the current official chapter in every real assessment.
